Plan New

The codex remembers your world, so you don't have to.

A sheet for every character, location, faction and item — each one a Markdown file in your binder. Memoire spots them in your prose as you write, maps where they turn up, and can fill a blank sheet from what you've already written.

Characters Locations Factions Items

A sheet per character — drafted from your pages.

Each kind starts with the fields writers reach for: role, appearance, goals, arc for a character; region and atmosphere for a place. Add your own, list aliases, link rivals and allies.

Left a field blank? Suggest from writings reads the passages where that character appears and drafts the missing pieces. You accept each one, or don't.

  • Per-binder codex, or a shared one every book can use
  • Rename an entry and carry the new name through your writings
  • Plain Markdown with YAML front matter — it syncs and versions like any note

On this device

Private by construction

Open GGUF models — Qwen, Llama, Mistral, Phi, DeepSeek R1 distills — download once and run offline. Metal on Apple Silicon, CPU elsewhere. Your writing has nowhere to go.

Or hosted

Bring a bigger model

Point it at OpenRouter with your own key and pick any model it offers. The key stays on your machine; switching back to local is a dropdown.

Or never

Leave it off

Leave the switch alone and Memoire stays a fast, plain editor. No model is fetched, no menu nags you to try it.

Backup & sync

Your backup is plain Markdown, somewhere you already know how to read.

Pick one home off-disk: a GitHub repo if you like history you can diff, a Dropbox folder if you'd rather never think about commits. Or neither — sync is optional.

Every save, a commit you own

Sign in with GitHub, name an owner/repo, and push with :sync — or let Memoire commit when you close it. Already have drafts on GitHub? Import pulls them down.

  • Plain Markdown commits — no proprietary format
  • Private repos; your token never leaves the machine
  • Works alongside the local branching history

On a Mac? One extra step the first time. +

Memoire isn't signed with an Apple Developer certificate yet, so macOS quarantines the download and says the app is damaged. It isn't. Drag it to Applications, then run this once in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/memoire.app

It removes the quarantine flag from that one app and changes nothing else. Sorry about this — once the developer account comes through, builds get notarized and this step goes away.

Questions writers ask

Where do my files actually live?+

In a folder on your disk (~/.memorie/notes by default, or wherever you choose). Binders are folders, writings are .md files, codex sheets are Markdown with a little YAML on top. Open them in any editor, today or in fifty years.

Does my writing get sent anywhere?+

Not unless you ask it to. There's no account and no telemetry. Sync only runs if you connect GitHub or Dropbox. AI is off by default; with a local model nothing leaves your machine, and with OpenRouter only what the assistant needs for your request is sent, under your own key.

Is it really free?+

Yes. Memoire is MIT-licensed open source. If you use a hosted model through OpenRouter, that's billed by OpenRouter to your key — local models cost nothing.

Can I move over from Scrivener or Word?+

Yes. Import reads Scrivener projects, Word documents, RTF and folders of Markdown (including Obsidian vaults). It keeps your order, never overwrites anything, and tells you about anything it couldn't convert.

What does a local model need?+

The default model is a small Qwen 2.5 (1.5B) that runs comfortably on a recent laptop — it downloads once, about 1.5 GB. Larger models are there if your machine can hold them. Apple Silicon uses Metal; everything else runs on the CPU.

Is it finished?+

It's young and moving fast — expect rough edges, and please report them. On the way: a mobile companion for reading and capture, margin notes and tracked changes for review, and a plugin API. No dates; follow the repo to watch them land.
